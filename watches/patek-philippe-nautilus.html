What to check before you buy

  • Papers and provenance: at this level, insist on the Patek Certificate of Origin or extract, and a clean ownership trail.
  • Authentication: buy from an established dealer and have an independent watchmaker open it.
  • Fakes and red flags: 'too good' prices, rushed wire transfers and sellers who won't meet or use escrow.

Alternatives worth a look

  • Patek Philippe Aquanaut: Patek's sportier sibling, in steel. (40.8 mm stainless steel, Calibre 26-330 S C, retail $27,257 (5167A-001))
  • Audemars Piguet Royal Oak Selfwinding: Genta's earlier integrated-bracelet icon. (41 mm stainless steel (15510ST), Calibre 4302, retail $31,900 (15510ST.OO.1320ST.06, AP US site))
  • Vacheron Constantin Overseas: A luxury steel sports watch that trades much closer to retail. (41 mm stainless steel, Calibre 5100, retail $29,200 (4520V/210A-B128))

History

Patek Philippe introduced the Nautilus in 1976, during the quartz crisis. The first model, ref. 3700, was in steel and designed by Gérald Genta, who had already designed AP's Royal Oak.

In 2006, for the collection's 30th anniversary, Patek introduced the 5711 and 5712, which became among its most popular models.

The current 5811/1G-001 is a 41 mm white gold reinterpretation with a blue sunburst, horizontally embossed dial and the self-winding 26-330 S C calibre.

FAQ

How much is a Patek Philippe Nautilus?

Patek's site lists the white gold 5811/1G-001 at $82,021 (checked Oct 19, 2026). On Chrono24, the middle of the market was asking about $199,950 to $248,300.

Is the steel Nautilus 5711 discontinued?

Yes. The 5811/1G in white gold is the current model in Patek's catalogue.

Who designed the Nautilus?

Gérald Genta, who also designed the Audemars Piguet Royal Oak. The first Nautilus, ref. 3700, came out in 1976.

What movement is in the 5811/1G?

The self-winding 26-330 S C with sweep seconds, date at 3 o'clock and a stop-seconds function.
