What to check before you buy

  • Box and papers: a brand warranty card matters for resale. Grey-market new watches may carry the dealer's warranty instead of the brand's, so ask which one you're getting.
  • Titanium: it's lighter and harder than ordinary titanium, but polished surfaces still pick up marks. Check the bracelet clasp.
  • Fakes and red flags: avoid prices far below the grey-market numbers above, stock photos only, or sellers who won't show the caseback, clasp and warranty card.

History

Grand Seiko started in 1960. It has been sold internationally since 2010, and in 2017 Seiko began presenting it as a separate brand, removing the Seiko name from new Grand Seiko dials.

The 'Snowflake' dial first appeared in October 2005 on the SBGA011. Grand Seiko says the pattern is inspired by wind-carved snow on the Hotaka mountains, visible from the Shinshu Watch Studio where Spring Drive watches are made.

The SBGA211 uses the Spring Drive Caliber 9R65 with about 72 hours of power reserve. The case and bracelet are high-intensity titanium, which Grand Seiko says is about 30% lighter than steel.

FAQ

How much is a Grand Seiko Snowflake?

Grand Seiko's US site lists the SBGA211 at $6,900 (checked Oct 19, 2026). A Chrono24 seller had it new at $6,617 plus shipping (Oct 28, 2026), and pre-owned asking prices on Chrono24 mostly ran about $5,050 to $6,800.

What is Spring Drive?

Grand Seiko's Spring Drive movement family. The SBGA211's Caliber 9R65 is self-winding with manual winding, rated ±15 seconds a month, and its seconds hand glides instead of ticking.

Is the Snowflake titanium?

Yes. The case and bracelet are high-intensity titanium, about 30% lighter than stainless steel, per Grand Seiko.

Why is it called Snowflake?

The dial texture is inspired by snow on the Hotaka mountains near the Shinshu Watch Studio, where Spring Drive watches are made.
