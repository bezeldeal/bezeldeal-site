What to check before you buy

  • Papers and provenance: insist on the Patek certificate and a matching serial.
  • Strap: the original composite strap wears with use. Check its condition and whether the original is included.
  • Fakes and red flags: buy from an established dealer and have it authenticated.

Alternatives worth a look

  • Patek Philippe Nautilus: The bigger-name Patek sports watch. (41 mm white gold (5811/1G), Calibre 26-330 S C, retail $82,021 (5811/1G-001))
  • Vacheron Constantin Overseas: Steel luxury sports watch with interchangeable straps, closer to retail. (41 mm stainless steel, Calibre 5100, retail $29,200 (4520V/210A-B128))
  • Audemars Piguet Royal Oak Selfwinding: The original steel luxury sports watch. (41 mm stainless steel (15510ST), Calibre 4302, retail $31,900 (15510ST.OO.1320ST.06, AP US site))

History

Patek Philippe introduced the Aquanaut in 1997 as a casual, sportier alternative to the Nautilus, aimed at younger buyers.

The first steel model, ref. 5060A, was 35.6 mm with a black checkerboard-style embossed dial and a 'Tropical' composite strap.

The 5167A is today's steel Aquanaut: a 40.8 mm case, an embossed charcoal dial and a strap in a matching composite material, powered by the self-winding 26-330 S C.
