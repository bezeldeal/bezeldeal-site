Which one I'd pick

New MT5400-U vs 2018 M79030N: the new one is thinner, Master Chronometer certified and has T-fit on every bracelet. The older M79030N is the value play: $4,150 new grey-market today, roughly $1,200 under a new bracelet BB58 at retail.

Black vs burgundy: black is the classic. The burgundy M7939A1A0RU-0001 is the bolder take, based on a Tudor prototype from the '90s that never went into production.

What to check before you buy

  • Ref matters: listings say 'Black Bay 58' for both generations. Check the ref (M79030N vs M7939...) before comparing prices.
  • Box and papers: a Tudor warranty card matters for resale. Grey-market new may carry the dealer's warranty instead of Tudor's five-year guarantee.
  • Fakes and red flags: popular Tudors get faked too. Avoid prices well under the pre-owned ranges above and sellers who won't show the caseback, the clasp and the card.

History

Tudor's ref. 7924 'Big Crown' of 1958 was the brand's first Submariner rated to 200 m, with an enlarged screw-down crown and a thicker case.

In 2018 Tudor introduced the Black Bay Fifty-Eight as a modern tribute to it: a 39 mm case, 200 m water resistance and the new COSC-certified Manufacture Calibre MT5402.

The new edition keeps the look but updates the case (11.7 mm thick), the bezel and crown, cuts the dial text to two lines, and moves to the MT5400-U, certified both COSC and METAS Master Chronometer, with a 65-hour power reserve and T-fit clasps on every bracelet and strap.

Fun fact

The '58' refers to 1958, the year of Tudor's ref. 7924 'Big Crown' diver.

FAQ

How much is a Tudor Black Bay 58?

Tudor's US site lists the current Black Bay 58 from $4,975 on rubber and $5,225 to $5,350 on bracelet (checked Oct 19, 2026). The previous M79030N-0001 was $4,150 new at Jomashop.

What's the difference between the old and new Black Bay 58?

The new BB58 (M7939 refs) uses the Master Chronometer MT5400-U with 65 hours of reserve, an 11.7 mm case, a redesigned bezel and crown, and T-fit clasps. The 2018 M79030N uses the COSC MT5402.

How thick is the Black Bay 58?

Tudor lists the current Black Bay 58 at 11.7 mm thick, 39 mm across, with 20 mm lugs.

Why is it called Black Bay 58?

It refers to 1958, when Tudor introduced the ref. 7924 'Big Crown', its first divers' watch rated to 200 m.

Is the Black Bay 58 a good first luxury watch?

It's a popular choice: 39 mm, 200 m, an in-house chronometer movement, and one of the few Swiss divers you can buy new under retail today.
